# Protecting family, food and shelter in a crisis — security without violence (English)

Oracle OS guide (Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish civil-protection guides, FEMA, Ready Scotland). The best defence is **not being a target**, **united neighbours** and **knowing when to leave**. Traps aimed at people are a crime almost everywhere and maim innocents (children, rescuers, neighbours) — don't use them.

## Low profile
Don't show or post about food, water, fuel or generators; minimise light and noise at night if looting is reported; **split supplies** across several hiding places (and with relatives elsewhere); keep a small "giveaway" stash.

## Harden the home
Locked doors and windows, quality lock/bar on the main door, film or bars on ground-floor windows, battery/solar motion lights, a dog. A **safe room** with a solid door, phone/radio, water, torch, first aid. Fire extinguisher and clear exits — fire is often a bigger risk than intruders.

## Community — the strongest protection
Know neighbours; organise **street watch** shifts, signals (whistle, PMR446 channel), meeting point; share water, cooking, care for the elderly and children, radio news. Volunteer with **civil protection, fire services, Red Cross** or official civil defence — that is how to defend your community and country lawfully.

## If someone threatens your family
**Your life is worth more than food.** Against weapons or groups, hand over what they want, observe, move away. Stay calm, hands visible, give them an exit. Children to the safe room; call **112 / 999 / 911** or use the radio; report afterwards. Self-defence must be **necessary and proportionate** to an ongoing attack; in the UK carrying weapons (including knives and sprays) for self-defence is illegal. Know your country's law.

## Know when to leave
If the area becomes lawless, waterless or a combat zone, a **planned evacuation** is the best defence: grab bag, agreed route, meeting point outside town, discreet note for relatives.
