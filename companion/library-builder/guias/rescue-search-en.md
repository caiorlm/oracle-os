# Search and rescue — rubble, fire, floods and lost people

Oracle OS guide. Based on FEMA CERT, INSARAG (UN), the US&R Field Operations Guide and RNLI (illustrated in the library: "FEMA CERT Basic Training", "INSARAG Guidelines vol. II", "US Urban SAR Field Operations Guide", "NWCG Incident Response Pocket Guide", "PHMSA Emergency Response Guidebook").

## Golden rule
**Don't become another casualty.** Many would-be rescuers die trying to save someone. Assess first, act second, always keep an exit.

## Size up the scene (before going in)
- What happened? How many people? Where could they be?
- Hazards: fire, smoke, gas (smell → get out, don't touch switches), electricity, water, chemicals (orange plates with numbers: look them up in the "Emergency Response Guidebook"), collapsing structures, aftershocks.
- Resources: who is with you, tools, light, radio, remaining daylight.
- Shut off gas and electricity at the meter if safe.

## Damaged buildings (earthquake, explosion, collapse)
- **Light damage** (cracks, glass): enter carefully.
- **Moderate damage** (cracked walls, damaged stairs): only quick entry for visible casualties.
- **Heavy damage** (fallen walls/roof, leaning, off foundation): **do not enter**. Mark it, cordon it, wait for professionals.
- Work in pairs, with helmet/gloves/dust mask and sturdy shoes. One person stays outside and knows where you are.

## Finding casualties
- **Call out and listen**: "If you can hear me, knock 3 times!" Then **total silence** for 1–2 minutes. Repeat from several points and triangulate.
- Search void spaces: under tables, along interior walls, stairwells, corners, vehicles.
- Surface and lightly trapped casualties first (most survivors are pulled out in the first hours by neighbours).
- **Marking** at the entrance (spray or chalk): an X — left: team; top: date and time; right: hazards; bottom: casualties (alive/dead). Prevents repeated searches.

## Freeing someone trapped
- **Lever and crib**: lift a little with a lever (bar, strong plank on a fulcrum), slide in a block; **lift an inch, crib an inch**. Never leave a load unsupported.
- Don't pull beams that hold other parts up. Remove debris by hand, passing it along a chain, top down.
- **Crush syndrome**: someone with a limb crushed for more than 1–2 hours can deteriorate suddenly when freed. Give water if conscious, get medical help, monitor; don't leave them alone.
- Protect the casualty from dust (cloth over face) and cold during extraction.

## Moving casualties
- Only for immediate danger. Suspected spinal injury: move as a unit, head in line.
- **Clothing drag** (by the shoulders of the top, head cradled on your forearms) or **blanket drag**.
- **Two-person carry**: hand seat, or one at the armpits and one at the knees.
- **Improvised stretcher**: two 2 m poles + coats with sleeves turned inside, or a blanket folded in thirds around the poles. Test with a healthy adult first.

## Fire and smoke
- Crawl below smoke; feel the door with the back of your hand before opening — hot, don't open.
- Close doors behind you (slows the fire). Trapped: close the door, seal gaps with wet cloth, signal from the window.
- Clothes on fire: **stop, drop, roll**. Cool burns 20 min with water.
- Carbon monoxide (generators, barbecues indoors): headache, nausea, drowsiness in several people → fresh air now.
- Wildfire: move to already-burnt ground, bare ground, water or a wide road; never run uphill ahead of the fire.

## Water — floods, rivers, ice
- **Reach, throw, row, go** — in that order. Reach with a stick, rope, clothing; throw something that floats (empty jerrycan, ball, life jacket). Only go in trained and with buoyancy.
- Moving water: **15 cm can knock an adult over, 30 cm can float a car**. "Turn around, don't drown."
- Ice: don't run to the casualty. Lie flat, spread your weight, push out a pole/ladder; pull them to the bank.
- See the "Water, rivers and sea" guide for currents, crossings and sea survival.

## Signals to be found
- **3 of anything** = distress: 3 whistle blasts, 3 fires in a triangle, 3 flashes. **SOS** in Morse: · · · — — — · · ·
- A whistle carries much further than a voice and uses less energy.
- **Ground-to-air signals** (large, 3 m+, contrasting): **V** = need help; **X** = need medical help; **N** = no; **Y** = yes; **arrow** = went this way.
- Mirror/shiny surface to flash sunlight at aircraft; bright colours; smoke by day (green leaves), fire by night.
- Radio: marine VHF channel 16; PMR446 channel 8 (code 0) and channel 1 are common emergency channels; say who, where, what, how many.

## Lost person
- If it's you: **STOP** — Stop, Think, Observe, Plan. Stay put if someone knows your route; shelter, signal.
- Children: teach them to "hug a tree" and stay still.
- Searching: start at the last seen point; check likely routes, junctions, water, shelters. Line search with people within sight of each other, calling and pausing to listen. Mark areas already searched.

## Reporting an emergency (radio or phone)
1. Who you are and where you're calling from. 2. **Where** (Oracle coordinates: Emergency tab). 3. What happened. 4. How many casualties and their state. 5. Hazards. 6. What you've done and what you need. Don't hang up first.

## Household rescue kit
Helmet or hard hat, work gloves, goggles, FFP2 masks, head torch + batteries, whistle, crowbar, hammer, saw, gas/water shut-off key, 15–30 m rope, barrier tape, chalk/spray, markers, blanket, first aid kit.
