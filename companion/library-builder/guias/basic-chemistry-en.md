# Basic chemistry for rebuilding — soap, lye, lime, charcoal, candles, vinegar, alcohol, ink and paper

Oracle OS guide. Manuals in the library: "Soap-Making Manual", "Handbook of Soap Manufacture", "Vegetable Dyes", LibreTexts Chemistry, Wikipedia. Wear gloves and eye protection and work outdoors: lye and lime burn skin and eyes (rinse 15 min with water if it happens).

## Wood-ash lye (potash) — the base of soap
1. **White** ash from hardwood (not paper, plastic or barbecue briquettes) in a bucket with holes in the bottom lined with straw.
2. Pour rainwater slowly; collect the brown liquid. Run it through the ash again until strong: **an egg or potato floats** showing a coin-sized patch above the surface.
3. Boil down to concentrate if needed. Store in glass or clay (not aluminium).

## Soap
- **Fat**: beef/mutton tallow, lard, olive or other oils; melt and strain.
- **Soft soap** (with ash lye = potassium): boil fat and lye, stirring for hours, until a honey-like paste with no fat on top. Test: a cooled dab should not strongly sting the tongue or feel greasy.
- **Hard soap**: add salt at the end (the paste separates and soap rises), or use bought caustic soda (weigh with a saponification table).
- Cure 4–6 weeks. Washing hands with soap prevents most diarrhoea and infections.

## Lime (mortar, limewash, disinfection, nixtamal)
- Burn limestone (or shells) in a kiln at ~900 °C for 1–3 days → quicklime. **Very caustic and heats with water**: slake slowly outdoors → slaked lime (putty). Store covered with water.
- Mortar: 1 part lime putty + 3 parts sand. Limewash: lime + water like milk — disinfects and protects walls. Lime in latrines cuts flies and smell.

## Charcoal
Stack dry wood, cover with earth/turf leaving vents, light from the centre; it smoulders 1–3 days with little air; when smoke turns thin and blue, seal everything and let it cool 1–2 days. Used in forges, water filters (crushed charcoal between sand) and smokeless cooking.

## Candles and light
Melted tallow or beeswax; dip a cotton wick repeatedly (dipped candles) or pour into a mould. Oil lamp: olive/vegetable oil in a dish with a wick. Never leave a flame unattended.

## Vinegar and preserving
Wine, cider or diluted honey fermented + vinegar "mother", in an open vessel covered with cloth, 3–6 weeks somewhere warm. Pickles vegetables and cleans.

## Alcohol (antiseptic and fuel)
- Ferment fruit, honey or malted grain with yeast (or sourdough) for 1–2 weeks.
- **Distil** in a still (closed pot + copper/glass tube cooled in water): **discard the first 5% (contains methanol, toxic)**. Disinfecting skin and instruments needs 60–80% alcohol; so does burning in lamps.
- Caution: flammable vapours — keep away from open flames.

## Salt
Evaporate seawater in trays in the sun or by boiling; strain through cloth first. Essential for preserving meat/fish and for livestock.

## Ink, paper and glue
- **Ink**: finely ground soot/charcoal + gum arabic or glue + water; or iron-gall ink (oak galls + iron sulphate made from scrap iron in vinegar).
- **Paper**: cotton/linen rag fibres or plants (nettle, straw) boiled with ash, beaten to pulp, spread in water and lifted on a mesh frame; press and dry.
- **Glue**: boil hides, bones or hooves for hours (animal glue); cooked starch (flour paste).

## Dyes
See "Textiles and leather" and "Vegetable Dyes": onion skins (yellow), walnut (brown), madder (red), indigo/woad (blue); alum or iron mordant.
