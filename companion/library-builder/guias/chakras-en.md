# Chakras — origins, the seven centres, nadis, Kundalini and how to meditate with them

Oracle OS guide. Based on the Hatha Yoga Pradipika and the Yoga Sutras (in the library), the *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa* (Pūrṇānanda, 1577) translated by Sir John Woodroffe ("Arthur Avalon") in *The Serpent Power* (1919), and historical scholarship on tantra.

## What they are — and what they are not
- *Cakra* is Sanskrit for "wheel" or "circle". In Hindu and Buddhist tantric texts (8th–16th centuries) they are **centres of concentration** visualised along the body's axis, used as a map for meditation, mantra and visualisation.
- **The number varies** by tradition: tantric Buddhism usually has 4 or 5; several Hindu texts give 6 + 1 (the best known today, from the *Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa*); others give more.
- The **rainbow colours** (red → violet) linked to 7 chakras are a 20th-century Western creation; the old texts describe different colours for each lotus.
- There is no scientific evidence that chakras are physical structures or that they cure disease. As an **attention and relaxation practice** they are safe and can calm. They don't replace medical treatment.

## The channels (nadis) and Kundalini
- **Sushumna**: central channel along the spine.
- **Ida** (left, lunar, calming) and **Pingala** (right, solar, activating) twist around Sushumna. Alternate-nostril breathing (Nadi Shodhana) aims to balance them.
- **Kundalini**: energy described as a sleeping serpent coiled at the base of the spine; in tantric practice it awakens and rises through Sushumna, piercing the chakras up to the crown.

## Similar systems elsewhere
- **Tibetan Buddhism**: channels (tsa), winds (lung) and drops (tigle); chakras at the crown, throat, heart and navel (sometimes the secret place), with the syllables OM, AH, HUM.
- **Taoism**: three **dantian** — lower (below the navel, vital energy), middle (heart, emotions) and upper (between the brows, spirit); the microcosmic orbit.
- **Sufism**: *lataif* (subtleties) at points in the chest and head, worked with dhikr.

## How to meditate with the chakras (20–30 minutes)
1. Sit upright, eyes closed, 10 slow belly breaths.
2. Bring attention to the **base of the spine**. Feel contact with the ground. If you like, chant "LAM" 3–7 times (or silently) feeling the vibration there. Imagine a light (red, or any colour you prefer) and the quality of **safety and stability**.
3. Move up to the **lower belly** — "VAM", flow. Then the **navel** — "RAM", calm strength. The **heart** — "YAM", tenderness and compassion (you can add loving-kindness). The **throat** — "HAM", true speech. **Between the brows** — "OM", clarity. The **crown** — silence, openness.
4. 1–3 minutes at each centre. At the end, imagine light running up the spine and back down, and rest in silence.
5. **Close by coming down**: bring attention back to the feet and the ground, move the hands, open the eyes. This prevents feeling "light-headed".

### Short versions
- **Spinal breath**: breathing in, feel attention rise from base to crown; breathing out, descend. 5 minutes.
- **Heart only**: hand on the chest, breathe into the centre of the chest, repeat "YAM" or a loving-kindness phrase.
- **Rooting** in moments of fear: feet on the ground, weight in the legs, slow breath, attention at the base of the body.

## Cautions
- Intense Kundalini practices (long breath retention, strong bandhas, hours of concentration) can cause dizziness, insomnia, anxiety, heat or uncontrolled energy sensations ("Kundalini crisis"). Do them only with an experienced teacher.
- Avoid breath retention with high blood pressure, heart disease, pregnancy, epilepsy or a history of psychosis.
- If physical symptoms appear (chest pain, breathlessness, numbness), treat them as medical, not as "energy".
